\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We construct synthetic cultural agents from declared aggregate preference anchors by fitting parameter-efficient adapters to sign-labeled pairwise comparisons. Evaluating these policies without country tokens makes the population-specific conditioning channel explicit. In our empirical application, the adapters learned the imposed pairwise labels, and on new trust items the fitted policies transferred the coarse GPS sign. However, they did not establish human response validity. While the adapters retained the training signal across survey text, the country prompt remained closer to human response distributions.

The next test is a blinded comparison of adapter-assisted item selection against expert selection, evaluated on an independent human pilot.
