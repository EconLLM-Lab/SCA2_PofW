\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The construction makes the rule translating declared aggregate preference signs into training labels explicit and auditable. A policy fitted to those labels can score new survey text without naming the population in the evaluation prompt, providing a controlled computational object for examining how supplied group information affects synthetic responses. This restriction applies to the declared conditioning channel, whereas pretrained representations and generator bank semantics remain embedded in the fitted model. We intend these policies to complement field work rather than replace human subjects or ascribe fixed cultural essences.

The empirical application demonstrates that anchor fidelity and human resemblance are distinct. Recovery of synthetic labels confirms that optimization moved pairwise contrasts in the intended direction, and the fitted policies transfer the coarse trust sign to unseen survey items. Distributional resemblance to human choices behaves differently: as shown in Section~\ref{sec:results}, prompting with country names produces a closer match to empirical human response distributions than the fitted adapters. Agreement with human country responses remains uncertain on this panel, while exploratory dimensions reveal substantial cross-survey divergence. The fitted policies are scoring instruments; construct validity requires independent human evidence.

A prospective application of this construction is survey pre-piloting, where researchers score candidate questionnaire items against declared benchmarks to nominate items before fielding. However, our results do not demonstrate that synthetic scoring reduces fielding costs, improves item selection, or preserves validity in practice. Prospective pre-pilot item screening still needs an independent human study. Specifically, evaluating this workflow requires a blinded comparison of adapter-assisted selection against expert selection on a held-out human pilot, following the validation design in Appendix~\ref{app:humanscreen}. Scoring candidate items with synthetic respondents cannot establish measurement validity, and empirical human data remain indispensable for calibrating and rectifying model-assisted measures \cite{krsteski2026}.

Several limitations bound this evidence. The country panel is a purposively selected development panel of sixteen economies rather than a probability sample of the joint survey intersection. Duplicated sign profiles mean that countries with the same six signs share the same training labels, and sign-only supervision discards anchor magnitudes. In addition, the scenarios rely on a single shared bank whose loadings reflect generator judgments rather than experimentally verified trait manipulations. Omitting country tokens at evaluation does not remove pretraining exposure to public surveys, and joint optimization across traits prevents attributing item responses exclusively to any single anchor dimension.
