\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The evaluation returns a specific profile of what holds along the chain from anchor to respondents, what fails, and what remains untested. We read it at the link between anchor and instrument, at the link between instrument and respondents, and as a contrast between two questions that the instrument answers differently, and we end with the limits that apply to all of it.

\paragraph{From anchor to instrument: what holds.} Trained at full scale, the instrument installs its synthetic labels on pairs it never saw. On held-out pairs the adapters' margins exceed the base model's on \cl{0.940}{C9_label_recovery_recovery_profile_block} of pairs, and the adapters prefer their own chosen completion on \cl{0.816}{C9_label_recovery_accuracy_profile_block} of them, against \cl{0.489}{C9_label_recovery_base_accuracy_profile_block} for the base model (Section~\ref{sec:res-recovery}). On trust the adapters also keep the GPS order of countries when the scenario bank is replaced, and an unseeded retraining of one profile leaves their readings nearly unchanged (Sections~\ref{sec:res-bank} and~\ref{sec:res-noise}). Naming the country does not recover that order, whereas a prompt that states the six signs does: the correlation with the GPS on trust is \cl{0.773}{PX_signprompt_trust_rho} \ci{0.584}{0.844}{PX_signprompt_trust_rho} for the sign prompt and \cl{0.028}{PX_persona_trust_rho} \ci{-0.339}{0.381}{PX_persona_trust_rho} for the named country (Section~\ref{sec:res-persona}). The registered contrast between the adapters and the sign prompt in the screen is unresolved (Section~\ref{sec:res-comparators}). What carries the trust order is therefore the declared sign, whichever channel supplies it, and the evidence cannot credit the trained weights over a prompt. The statement concerns trust. Success on one dimension does not establish a general account of weights against prompts, and it is silent on whether the WVS items measure the GPS constructs in people.

\paragraph{Why the signs carry the trust order.} The behavioral decomposition shows where the trust order survives (Section~\ref{sec:res-w1}). We split the adapters' main effects into the part due to each surface's own sign and the part due to the other five signs. The own part orders countries like the GPS on every surface, with correlations from \cl{0.817}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_spearman_own_vs_gps} to \cl{0.866}{C9_W1_posrecip_spearman_own_vs_gps}. Because every own effect is positive, that part restates the correlation between the signs and the GPS, and it is a property of the labels. What differs across surfaces is how much the other five signs add. On trust their part carries a small share of the variation of the full prediction, \cl{0.147}{C9_W1_trust_cross_var_share}, and the full prediction keeps the order of countries, at \cl{0.834}{C9_W1_trust_spearman_full_vs_gps}. On the other six surfaces their part carries most of the variation, with a ratio of variances from \cl{0.830}{C9_W1_negrecip_cross_var_share} to \cl{1.049}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_cross_var_share}, and the full prediction loses most of the order. Trust is thus the one surface on which the other signs leave the ordering by the own sign largely intact. The decomposition describes the adapters' behavior and locates nothing inside the network. It sets one surface against six, so we claim no graded relation, and the labels themselves do not depend on a second dimension.

\paragraph{Entanglement.} The factorial shows that the declared dimensions are entangled inside the instrument. Each sign moves its own surface in the intended direction and also moves the surfaces of other dimensions, and one common direction carries about half of the variance across surfaces. The planned separability check fails on both routes. Prompting with the same signs is more entangled still: one common direction dominates, the trust sign moves every surface, and three own effects point the wrong way. The same pattern appears on the held-out items, where the prompted probe assigned its selected items to trust alone and did not pass the null. Training on sign-labeled pairs is thus the route that keeps the direction of each declared input, and it does not deliver separate dimensions. Entanglement under intervention has been reported for activation steering \citep{dangmasud2026}. What the factorial adds is a joint measurement for trained adapters and for a prompt that carry the same declared information, so the contrast describes two instruments and makes no claim about the best training recipe or about screening accuracy.

\paragraph{From instrument to respondents.} The screen asked whether a probe that reads only the instrument picks items whose human country differences run in the assigned direction, and the registered answer is yes, weakly (Section~\ref{sec:res-screen}). The signal is a small fraction of what two human waves achieve on the same items, its bootstrap interval reaches zero, and it is concentrated in risk taking. That dimension is the one the second bank does not support, and its interval does not survive an exploratory adjustment for the four dimensions tested. Trust, which does survive the second bank, does not stand apart from zero in the screen. The items assigned to positive reciprocity point the wrong way. That disagreement could arise from the instrument, from the mapping between WVS items and the GPS construct, or from limited coherence between the two surveys for this dimension, and the design cannot separate these explanations. On the items that the adapters share with two readers of the questionnaire, the registered contrasts cannot tell them apart, and the overlap is small. The follow-up analyses agree (Section~\ref{sec:res-human}). The adapters' order of countries does not detectably agree with the respondents' composite on any of seven surfaces, and the GPS's own agreement with that composite is loose on these items. We do not test whether the anchor or the instrument accounts for the weak agreement between adapters and respondents, and the design cannot separate the two.

\paragraph{Order and distribution.} The two questions separated in the introduction receive different answers (Section~\ref{sec:res-tvd}). The adapters keep the anchor's order of countries on trust and lie farther from the survey's answer distributions than the base model, by \cl{0.045}{C9_TVD_adapter_minus_base_profile_block} \ci{0.006}{0.093}{C9_TVD_adapter_minus_base_profile_block} in total variation distance. A country-named prompt lies closer, by \cl{-0.105}{C9_TVD_persona_minus_base_profile_block} \ci{-0.122}{-0.086}{C9_TVD_persona_minus_base_profile_block}, and carries no trust order. If agreement were one quantity, the arms would fall in one ranking, and they fall in two. The training signal suggests a reading that we offer without testing. It consists of signs, so it states which of two responses is preferred and says nothing about how strongly, or about how widely respondents spread across the answer options. The order of countries is a quantity that signs speak to. A distance to the survey's answer distributions is a quantity they carry no information about, and the instrument was not built to reduce it. We do not test why the distributions move apart: the sign labels carry no magnitudes, the readout is a scoring convention, and the base model is shared, and the design cannot separate these. The contrast describes two questions on one anchor, one instrument family and one survey. It does not show that the country-named prompt matches respondents, since the same prompt has no trust order. Elsewhere a country name has been found to recover a country ranking in the European Social Survey while supporting no distributional inference \citep{wangc2026}, so the persona result here is a statement about the trust order of one benchmark and should not be extended beyond it. The separation of group-level order from distributional fidelity is itself established \citep{dasilva2026}. The present evidence adds a case in which the two cross. It is descriptive, it was not preregistered, and it earns no claim.

\paragraph{Limits.} Five limits apply to every result above. First, the human criterion is retrospective. The WVS Wave~7 file existed before the probe, the pool and the adapters, and it is the only human source. Registration and the freeze guard protect against tuning the probe to the criterion, but they cannot make the criterion independent of the survey program from which the item pool was drawn. Pre-piloting, in the sense that screening with the instrument would change which items a researcher fields relative to a human-only rule declared in advance, is a prospective use that this paper does not test. Second, the instrument is built from signs. It carries no information about magnitudes, and it cannot represent differences among countries that share a sign profile or among people within a country. Its readings are group-level, and country-level agreement does not establish individual-level validity or measurement invariance \citep{davidov2014,bratt2025}. Third, every adapter shares one base model, one training recipe and one readout convention, and the readout's code probabilities are a scoring convention whose dispersion need not match human response distributions. Fourth, the per-dimension cells are small and the selected set churns under readout variants, so the instrument's item-level assignments are more stable than any particular list of selected items. Fifth, the noise checks are narrow: one unseeded retrain bounds retraining noise for one unit, and two near-zero refits are a screen with no claim across countries.

\paragraph{Intended use.} Within those limits, the instrument has a modest and inspectable use. A survey designer can read the dimension and direction it assigns to candidate items as one input beside expert review and human pretesting, keeping in view that no dimension passed both checks. Its value lies in what it makes explicit: the declared moments, the sign rule, and the absence of any group name at evaluation are all on the record, so a reader can see what information the instrument was given and check where its readings depart from people. As a complement to fielding and pretesting it has a place, and the evidence here gives no reason to use it in their place. In the terms of \citet{broska2025}, its readings are potentially informative observations that still require a human criterion.
