\section{Evaluation Design}\label{sec:design}

The evaluation separates what each comparison permits. A construction check asks whether the adapters encode what they were trained on. A second-bank check asks whether that encoding survives a change of scenario text. A held-out screen asks whether the instrument's readings of new items agree with an existing human survey. A factorial diagnostic asks whether the declared dimensions behave as separate inputs. We report every number with a label for the surface it was computed on (Table~\ref{tab:surfaces}), and numbers from different surfaces are never pooled.

\subsection{Second-bank check}\label{sec:design-bank}

For each surface of the strict battery, we compute the Spearman correlation between the adapters' surface composites and the GPS coordinate of the surface's own dimension over the \dc{42}{countries} countries, first for the first cohort (S1) and then for the second cohort with each country assigned its profile's adapter (S2). Intervals come from a profile-block bootstrap that resamples the \dc{26}{profiles_realized} sign profiles realized by the countries, with \dc{2,000}{boot_draws} draws, seed \dc{20261011}{seed_c3}, and percentile limits. The first-cohort values reproduce the banked run exactly. The imprint set is the \dc{4}{imprint_dims} dimensions whose first-cohort interval excluded zero: trust, risk taking, positive reciprocity and altruism. Patience and negative reciprocity were flat in the first cohort and are reported without interpretation. The rule was fixed before the second cohort was trained: an imprint dimension whose second-bank interval includes zero is named, and no sentence about that dimension carrying over to the second bank is written. We also report the agreement between the two banks over the \dc{26}{profiles_realized} profiles they share, as the Pearson correlation between second-bank profile composites and first-bank means over the countries in each profile, with a \dc{2,000}{boot_draws}-draw bootstrap over profiles. Bank-to-bank agreement measures how much the scenario text changes the instrument; it involves no human data.

\subsection{The held-out screen}\label{sec:design-screen}

\paragraph{Item pool.} The pool was built from WVS Wave~7 metadata alone (variable labels, value labels and questionnaire text). An item enters if it is a substantive question outside the battery used to develop the adapters, below the demographic block, with two to eleven contiguous ordinal response codes. A text review excluded items whose code order is not monotone or that test factual knowledge, confirmed the ordinal reading of others, and repaired wording that did not extract from the questionnaire, recording a reason for every change. The result is \dc{223}{pool_items} items that no adapter was built for.

\paragraph{Probe.} All \dc{64}{cells} second-cohort adapters scored every pool item under the frozen specification. For each item $i$, the probe regresses the \dc{64}{cells} adapter scores on an intercept, the six sign bits coded $\pm\dc{0.5}{x_code}$, and their \dc{15}{interactions} two-way interactions, which leaves \dc{42}{error_df} error degrees of freedom. With $t_{d}$ the main-effect $t$ statistic of dimension $d$, the item's assigned dimension is $d_i=\arg\max_d|t_d|$, or none when the largest $|t_d|$ is below \dc{2.0}{t_threshold}, and its sign is $s_i=\operatorname{sign}(b_{d_i})$. The primary rule keeps items whose assigned dimension is in the imprint set and whose $|t|$ is at least \dc{2}{t_threshold}, up to the top \dc{25}{top_share} percent of all pool items by $|t|$, which caps the selection at \dc{56}{max_selected} items. The secondary rule keeps every imprint item above the fixed threshold; it was declared a reliability check and does not enter the inference. The probe reads model scores only. It was stated in advance that, under pure noise, about a quarter of items cross the threshold on at least one dimension; the selection rule does not control this rate, and the permutation null below absorbs it.

\paragraph{Statistic and criterion.} For each selected item, the signed item agreement is
\begin{equation}
a_i = s_i\cdot\rho\big(\bar h_{ci},\,z_{c,d_i}\big),
\qquad
A=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}a_i,
\label{eq:screen}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the Spearman correlation over the \dc{42}{countries} countries, $\bar h_{ci}$ is the survey-weighted WVS Wave~7 mean of item $i$ in country $c$ with negative codes set to missing, and $S$ is the selected set. An item--country cell that was never fielded enters as missing and is not imputed. $A$ is positive when the human country differences on the selected items run in the direction the instrument assigned, against the GPS coordinate of the assigned dimension.

\paragraph{Inference.} The registered test is a permutation null over items: draw as many items as were selected uniformly from the pool, give them the selected items' dimension and sign pairs in random order, and recompute $A$; \dc{10,000}{perm_draws} draws, seed \dc{20261015}{seed_screen}, one-sided $p$ equal to the share of null draws at or above the observed $A$. Because the null redraws items from the same pool, it absorbs the rate at which the probe flags noise items. A \dc{95}{ci_level} percent interval for $A$ comes from \dc{2,000}{boot_draws} bootstrap resamples of the \dc{42}{countries} countries with percentile limits. The registered readings were fixed in advance: if $A$ is not above the null at one-sided $\alpha=\dc{0.05}{alpha}$, the adapters do not screen held-out items better than random assignment; if $A$ is above the null but below one third of the human ceiling, a screening signal exists but is weak.

\paragraph{Human ceiling.} For each selected item fielded in both WVS waves 6 and 7, we computed the Spearman correlation between the two waves' country means over the \dc{28}{ceiling_countries} countries with both waves \citep{wvsts2022} and averaged it over items. This is the agreement two human surveys reach on these items. The ratio of $A$ to the ceiling is a benchmark only, because the two statistics are built differently: $A$ correlates one item with an anchor over \dc{42}{countries} countries, while the ceiling correlates one item with itself across waves over fewer countries.

\paragraph{Per-dimension readings.} The registered analysis also reports $A_d$, the mean of $a_i$ over the selected items assigned to dimension $d$, with an interval from the same \dc{2,000}{boot_draws} country resamples, and the share of items with $a_i>0$. An imprint dimension whose interval includes zero is read as giving no evidence of agreement for that dimension. These intervals are unadjusted for testing four dimensions. As an exploratory analysis added after the registered results were read, we report Bonferroni intervals over the \dc{4}{imprint_dims} imprint dimensions, read off the same bootstrap draws at the \dc{98.75}{bonf_level} percent level.

\paragraph{Comparators.} Two comparators read only the questionnaire. The face-valid rule assigns an item to a dimension when exactly one dimension's construct words occur in its wording and reads the direction from the response labels; a person could only confirm, flip or abstain, with a written reason. The second comparator is a typed decision model that returns calibrated probabilities (jev-1.13.0), given the item wording, the response options and the six GPS construct definitions used to generate the training data; because single calls were unstable near the decision threshold, the comparator of record is \dc{5}{jev_runs} independent runs, and an item is assigned only when at least \dc{3}{jev_majority} runs agree on dimension and sign. Neither comparator saw any model score or human response. The registered primary contrast is coverage-matched: $D_{\mathrm{common}}=A_{\mathrm{adapter}}-A_{\mathrm{comparator}}$ on the items both the adapter selection and the comparator assign, with an interval from the paired country bootstrap. An interval above zero would mean the adapters find structure the wording does not reveal; an interval that covers zero was registered as ``the adapters match a careful reader''; an interval below zero would favor the reader's rule. The contrast on each route's own items is reported beside it. Both comparators concentrate on trust, which the preregistration named in advance as a limitation.

\paragraph{Readout variants and the prompt route.} The probe was re-estimated on three re-scorings of the pool: options in reversed order, and two paraphrases of the prompt template. The registered statistic is the share of selected items whose dimension and sign are unchanged; the selection was declared dependent on wording if more than half change. The same probe and test were also applied to the \dc{64}{cells} prompted sign profiles.

\subsection{Registration, blinding and amendments}\label{sec:design-prereg}

The held-out screen was registered in a document committed to the project repository and timestamped by the pushed git tag \texttt{prereg-heldout-2026-10-14}, before any held-out item was scored by an adapter. At that point no selection existed and no held-out human value had been read; the only WVS Wave~7 content read was metadata and questionnaire text. A freeze guard enforced the order of work. After the probe selection, a freeze file recorded the hashes of the selection and the item pool, and every program that reads held-out human data refuses to run unless that file exists and both hashes still match. The order was: score the held-out items, run the probe, freeze, and only then join the human data. The freeze was committed a few minutes before the first read of held-out human data.

Two amendments were made, both before any human held-out value was read (Figure~\ref{fig:timeline}). The first scoring pass ran on the pool as registered. Reading the item text afterwards found \dc{12}{repaired_items} items whose standalone wording could not be answered as written (sentences cut off mid-clause, a stripped country placeholder, grid rows truncated to a pronoun, and follow-ups that refer to a question absent from a standalone prompt). Amendment~1 repaired these items from their own response options or variable labels, under the pool rule and override mechanism already registered, and every adapter, readout variant and prompted profile was re-scored under the amended specification as a single run. We disclose that the first probe pass had been computed and read before the repairs were chosen, and that two of the repaired items (Q153 and Q157) were in its top quartile. The comparators had assigned all pool items on the pre-repair wording. Amendment~2 removed the repaired items from both comparator routes before any comparator statistic was computed; this affected one item (Q82), which was not in the adapter selection. The decision was taken with the instruction to freeze, before any human value was read; the amendment text was written after the primary statistic was read and records that earlier decision.

The criterion is retrospective. The WVS Wave~7 file is public and existed before the probe, the pool and the adapters, and it is the only human source in this study. Registration fixed the analysis before the human values were read by us, which guards against tuning the probe to the criterion. It cannot make the criterion prospective, and the screen tests agreement with an existing survey. Results on surface H are therefore labelled retrospective throughout. Every scoring run reproduced its pinned specification hash before a model was loaded. All manifests of the held-out runs record one code revision; launcher files around it were patched in place during a recovery, and the scoring modules are byte-identical between the patched setup and the recorded revision. The strict-battery run of the second cohort spans two recorded revisions. The online supplement lists the revisions, their differences in the readout path and the file hashes, and it reports two provenance checks. The commit that produced the first cohort's scores cannot be named, and a replay traced the dominant difference between those scores and an unquantized replay to the four-bit quantization the adapters were trained on. A report-only comparison of base-model scores with the archived scores of the earlier study shows a gap whose remaining cause is not established, and no statistic in this article uses those archived scores.

\subsection{The sign-profile factorial}\label{sec:design-factorial}

Because the second cohort covers all \dc{64}{cells} sign profiles, the strict-battery composites of its adapters form a full factorial in six binary factors. For each of the seven surfaces we fit the same regression as the probe (intercept, six sign bits coded $\pm\dc{0.5}{x_code}$, \dc{15}{interactions} two-way interactions, \dc{42}{error_df} error degrees of freedom). A main effect is then the difference between the mean composite of profiles with the bit set and profiles without it. We call it the main effect of the sign bit on the adapter's scores. This is a causal statement about the instrument, because the bits were assigned by design. The manipulation acts on synthetic training labels, and its effects are changes in the model's response distributions. They carry no implication about how preferences operate in people.

Each bit has an own surface (patience owns two), so there are seven own effects. The project's analysis plan, separate from the held-out registration, set one acceptance check for the factorial: the factorial is informative about separability if at least \dc{4}{v5_required} of the six bits move their own surface more than any other surface, a criterion we call V5, with a binomial reference that takes the chance of a hit as one in seven. Because raw effects compare surfaces with different response scales, we add two scale-free views as exploratory analyses: the same comparison on effects divided by each surface's standard deviation over the \dc{64}{cells} profiles, and a column-wise count of surfaces whose largest mover among the six bits is their own bit. We also report the share of variance of the standardized composites on their first principal component and the mean absolute correlation between surfaces. The same analyses on the \dc{64}{cells} prompted profiles give the route contrast, which compares two instruments carrying the same declared information.

\subsection{Retraining noise and the near-zero margin}\label{sec:design-noise}

One profile adapter was retrained under the same protocol as an unseeded retrain, providing a noise-floor estimate for the pipeline. The low-rank initialization of the training code is not seeded, so the retrain shares the recipe and the data and differs in its random initialization. We compare its scores with the original on both batteries.

The sign rule treats a coordinate just above zero like a large positive one. A sensitivity check fixed before any refit was trained removes the training pairs of every dimension whose GPS coordinate lies within \dc{0.10}{near_zero_cut} of zero and refits two predeclared countries (Great Britain and Colombia), each beside a size-matched control trained on the same number of pairs drawn at random. The contrast of interest is the refit minus its control on the dimensions still in training, in units of the surface's standard deviation over the \dc{64}{cells} profiles, read against the retrain's shift on the same surface as a noise floor. The predeclared rule was to report the margin beside the main results and never to promote it.
