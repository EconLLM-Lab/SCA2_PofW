Dear Editor,

We submit "Synthetic Cultural Agents as Measurement Instruments: A Preregistered Evaluation from Cross-National
Anchor to Survey Respondents" for consideration in *Sociological Methods & Research*.

The article evaluates a scoring instrument for new survey items, built by compiling the signs of declared
country-level preference benchmarks into fine-tuned language-model policies, against an existing cross-national
survey. It reports what holds, what fails and what remains untested: a preregistered retrospective screen of
held-out World Values Survey items, a second-scenario-bank check, a factorial diagnostic of whether the declared
dimensions separate, and comparisons with a prompt that states the same signs and a prompt that names the country.

**Prior posting.** An earlier version of this work was posted as a working paper on arXiv (arXiv:2610.06562,
October 2026). The submitted article is the journal version of that paper. It replaces the sixteen-country
development panel with every country covered by both benchmarks, adds a second training bank, the preregistered
held-out screen, the factorial diagnostic and the prompt comparisons, and re-scores every comparison on one frozen
software stack. The anonymized manuscript does not cite the preprint, so that the review stays double-anonymized.
