\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

We report the second-bank check first, because it says what the instrument encodes before any human data enter. The retrospective screen follows, then its comparators and readout checks, then the factorial diagnostic and the noise checks. A last part reports descriptive follow-up analyses that were not preregistered: agreement with respondents, distance to the survey distributions, a decomposition of the factorial's cross-talk, and held-out label recovery (surface D). Each number carries the label of its surface.

\subsection{A second scenario bank}\label{sec:res-bank}

The first cohort encodes four of the six declared signs on the strict battery (S1). Over the \dc{42}{countries} countries, the adapter--GPS Spearman correlation is \cl{0.744}{B2_cohort1_trust_rho} \ci{0.500}{0.865}{B2_cohort1_trust_rho} for trust, \cl{0.435}{B2_cohort1_risktaking_rho} \ci{0.085}{0.686}{B2_cohort1_risktaking_rho} for risk taking, \cl{0.488}{B2_cohort1_posrecip_rho} \ci{0.120}{0.695}{B2_cohort1_posrecip_rho} for positive reciprocity, and \cl{0.406}{B2_cohort1_altruism_rho} \ci{0.020}{0.676}{B2_cohort1_altruism_rho} for altruism. These four are the imprint set that the held-out screen later uses. The online supplement reports the trust correlation on the sixteen countries of the development stage.

When the scenario bank is replaced (S2, first column of Table~\ref{tab:checks}), trust keeps its agreement with the GPS, at \cl{0.803}{B2_bank2_trust_rho} \ci{0.615}{0.876}{B2_bank2_trust_rho}. The other three imprint dimensions keep positive point estimates, but their intervals now include zero, so \cl{1}{B2_n_imprint_kept} of the \cl{4}{B2_n_imprint_kept:n} imprint dimensions passes the second-bank check and \cl{3}{B2_n_imprint_dropped} do not. Under the rule fixed before the second cohort was trained, we write no sentence about risk taking, positive reciprocity or altruism carrying over to the second bank. The surfaces that were flat on the first bank stay flat on the second: negative reciprocity \cl{0.239}{B2_bank2_negrecip_rho} \ci{-0.237}{0.604}{B2_bank2_negrecip_rho}, patience Q43 \cl{0.005}{B2_bank2_patience:Q43_rho} \ci{-0.403}{0.470}{B2_bank2_patience:Q43_rho}, and patience Q50 \cl{0.161}{B2_bank2_patience:Q50_rho} \ci{-0.343}{0.557}{B2_bank2_patience:Q50_rho}.

The two banks agree closely on most surfaces when compared profile by profile. Over the \dc{26}{profiles_realized} shared profiles, the Pearson correlation between first-bank and second-bank composites is \cl{0.909}{B2_bank_to_bank_trust_pearson} \ci{0.797}{0.965}{B2_bank_to_bank_trust_pearson} for trust, \cl{0.949}{B2_bank_to_bank_altruism_pearson} \ci{0.859}{0.989}{B2_bank_to_bank_altruism_pearson} for altruism, \cl{0.898}{B2_bank_to_bank_posrecip_pearson} \ci{0.775}{0.960}{B2_bank_to_bank_posrecip_pearson} for positive reciprocity, \cl{0.708}{B2_bank_to_bank_negrecip_pearson} \ci{0.488}{0.861}{B2_bank_to_bank_negrecip_pearson} for negative reciprocity, and \cl{0.598}{B2_bank_to_bank_risktaking_pearson} \ci{0.229}{0.819}{B2_bank_to_bank_risktaking_pearson} for risk taking. Risk taking is the least stable surface across banks, which fits its loss on the second-bank check. For altruism and positive reciprocity the profile composites of the two banks are highly correlated, and the point estimates of agreement with the GPS fall only modestly; their intervals cross zero because they were already wide. Bank-to-bank agreement measures how much the scenario text changes the instrument. It says nothing about people.

\subsection{Naming the country}\label{sec:res-persona}

The comparison a survey researcher would reach for first is a prompt that names the population. Scored on the same stack, the country-named persona route does not reproduce the GPS trust order (P): its correlation over the \dc{42}{countries} countries is \cl{0.028}{PX_persona_trust_rho} \ci{-0.339}{0.381}{PX_persona_trust_rho}. The planned contrast with the first cohort is \cl{0.716}{PX_D_cohort1_trust} \ci{0.191}{1.154}{PX_D_cohort1_trust}, so on trust the adapters track the benchmark's order more closely than the named-country prompt, and the reading is the same under a country bootstrap. The sign-profile prompt, which states the same six signs in words, tracks trust as closely as the adapters, at \cl{0.773}{PX_signprompt_trust_rho} \ci{0.584}{0.844}{PX_signprompt_trust_rho}. On trust, what separates the routes is whether the declared sign is supplied, whichever channel carries it. The contrast therefore sets declared signs against a country name, and it says nothing about trained weights against prompting. On the other surfaces the persona route carries part of the patience ordering, positively on one item and negatively on the other, and its remaining intervals include zero. The online supplement reports every surface and the differences for both cohorts and the sign prompt.

\subsection{The retrospective screen}\label{sec:res-screen}

Retrospectively, the screen rejects its registered null. The probe selected \cl{56}{SCR_A_primary:n} of the \dc{223}{pool_items} pool items under the primary rule. Their mean signed agreement with the WVS Wave~7 country differences is $A=\cl{0.061}{SCR_A_primary}$, with a country-bootstrap interval of \ci{-0.007}{0.126}{SCR_A_primary}. Under the permutation null, which reassigns the same dimension and sign pairs to items drawn at random from the pool, the null distribution has mean \cl{0.012}{SCR_null_mean} and ninety-fifth percentile \cl{0.046}{SCR_null_q95}, and the one-sided $p$ is \cl{0.010}{SCR_p_perm}. Of the selected items, \cl{60.7}{SCR_share_a_pos:pct}\% have a positive item agreement. The human data contain \cl{174}{SCR_cells_not_fielded} item--country cells that were never fielded; they enter as missing.

By the registered reading, the signal is weak. Two WVS waves agree with each other on the selected items fielded in both waves with a mean Spearman correlation of \cl{0.746}{SCR_ceiling_mean} (median \cl{0.767}{SCR_ceiling_median}, \cl{34}{SCR_ceiling_mean:n} items). The screen's $A$ is \cl{8.1}{SCR_A_over_ceiling:pct}\% of that ceiling, well below the registered bar of one third, which would be \cl{0.249}{SCR_one_third_ceiling}. The ratio is a benchmark, because the two statistics are built differently. The bootstrap interval reaches zero while the permutation test rejects the null, and the two answer different questions. The permutation test asks whether the instrument's assignments beat random assignments of the same pairs to items from the same pool, and it absorbs the rate at which the probe flags noise items. The bootstrap asks how much $A$ would vary across samples of countries. The direction of the finding is therefore clearer than its size.

The registered reliability checks point the same way. The secondary rule, which keeps every imprint item above the fixed threshold, gives $A=\cl{0.070}{SCR_secondary_A}$ over \cl{185}{SCR_secondary_A:n} items, with a one-sided permutation $p\cl{<0.001}{SCR_secondary_p_perm}$ (null ninety-fifth percentile \cl{0.034}{SCR_secondary_null_q95}). Dropping one country at a time moves the primary $A$ between \cl{0.051}{SCR_loco_min} and \cl{0.072}{SCR_loco_max}. In an exploratory check on the \cl{34}{SCR_A_wave6:n} items fielded in both waves, $A$ is \cl{0.043}{SCR_A_wave6} against wave-6 country means and \cl{0.047}{SCR_A_wave7} against wave-7 means over the \dc{28}{ceiling_countries} countries with both waves, so the small signal does not depend on one wave.

\subsection{Readout robustness}\label{sec:res-readout}

Item-level assignment is stable under option order and paraphrase, whereas membership in the particular selected set churns. The registered statistic, the number of selected items whose dimension and sign are unchanged, is \cl{39}{RO_heldout_reverse_ds_unchanged} of \cl{56}{RO_heldout_reverse_ds_unchanged:n} with reversed options, \cl{39}{RO_heldout_para_a_ds_unchanged} with the first paraphrase, and \cl{30}{RO_heldout_para_b_ds_unchanged} with the second. No variant changes more than half, so the registered condition for wording dependence is not met, although the second paraphrase comes close. Membership is a different matter, because the primary rule is a top-quartile cut on $|t|$ and small shifts reorder items near the cut. Only \cl{18}{RO_heldout_reverse_membership_agreement}, \cl{29}{RO_heldout_para_a_membership_agreement} and \cl{16}{RO_heldout_para_b_membership_agreement} of the \cl{56}{RO_heldout_para_b_membership_agreement:n} items are selected under both the original readout and the respective variant, and the correlation of $|t|$ between the original and each variant over the pool is \cl{0.176}{RO_heldout_reverse_abs_t_corr}, \cl{0.546}{RO_heldout_para_a_abs_t_corr} and \cl{0.271}{RO_heldout_para_b_abs_t_corr}. A researcher who reads the instrument should therefore trust the dimension and direction it assigns to an item more than the particular list of items it ranks highest.

\subsection{Why trust is spared: a behavioral decomposition}\label{sec:res-w1}

The factorial shows that each bit moves its own surface in the intended direction and also moves other surfaces (Section~\ref{sec:res-factorial}). To ask why the adapters keep the GPS order of countries on trust and lose it on most other surfaces, we split the main effects of the second-cohort adapters into the effect of each surface's own bit and the summed effect of the other five bits. We evaluate each part at the sign profile of each of the \dc{42}{countries} countries and correlate it with the GPS coordinate of the surface's own dimension (online supplement gives every surface). This is a behavioral decomposition of the adapters' main effects. It says nothing about mechanism inside the network.

The own-bit part orders the countries like the GPS on all seven surfaces, with correlations from \cl{0.817}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_spearman_own_vs_gps} to \cl{0.866}{C9_W1_posrecip_spearman_own_vs_gps}. This is mechanical. For a positive own effect, as all seven are, the own part is proportional to the sign of the GPS coordinate, so it restates the correlation between the signs and the GPS and is not a separate test of learning. The full prediction, which adds the cross bits, keeps the GPS order on trust, \cl{0.834}{C9_W1_trust_spearman_full_vs_gps}, and loses most of it elsewhere, with correlations from \cl{-0.020}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_spearman_full_vs_gps} for patience Q43 to \cl{0.422}{C9_W1_altruism_spearman_full_vs_gps} for altruism. The cross part alone correlates \cl{0.574}{C9_W1_trust_spearman_cross_vs_gps} with the GPS on trust and between \cl{-0.195}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_spearman_cross_vs_gps} and \cl{0.285}{C9_W1_altruism_spearman_cross_vs_gps} on the other six.

The cross part carries little of the variation of the full prediction on trust and most of it elsewhere. Var(cross)/Var(full) is \cl{0.147}{C9_W1_trust_cross_var_share} on trust and runs from \cl{0.830}{C9_W1_negrecip_cross_var_share} to \cl{1.049}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_cross_var_share} on the other six. This is a ratio of variances and not a percentage of variance explained. It exceeds one on patience Q43 because the own and cross parts covary negatively. In covariance terms the cross part's share is \cl{0.287}{C9_W1_trust_cross_cov_share} on trust and runs from \cl{0.727}{C9_W1_negrecip_cross_cov_share} to \cl{0.985}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_cross_cov_share} elsewhere. The main effects carry between \cl{0.483}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_main_effect_share_64} and \cl{0.925}{C9_W1_altruism_main_effect_share_64} of the variance of the surfaces over the \dc{64}{cells} profiles. Interactions are needed on four surfaces, and they do not restore the GPS order: with two-way terms added, the correlations with the GPS run from \cl{-0.066}{C9_W1_patience_Q43_spearman_main_plus_2way_vs_gps} to \cl{0.331}{C9_W1_risktaking_spearman_main_plus_2way_vs_gps}. Across the seven surfaces, the rank correlation between Var(cross)/Var(full) and the adapters' failure to order countries like the GPS is \cl{0.821}{C9_W1_rank_cross_var_share_vs_failure_bank2} against second-cohort composites and \cl{0.286}{C9_W1_rank_cross_var_share_vs_failure_cohort1} against first-cohort composites. It rests on one surface, trust, set against six, so it is not a graded relation and we claim no dose--response. No bank label depends on a second dimension: in both banks all \cl{27{,}636}{C9_W1_bank1_label_rows_checked} country-pair training rows follow the sign of the pair's own dimension, with \cl{0}{C9_W1_bank1_label_mismatches} mismatches in the first bank and \cl{0}{C9_W1_bank2_label_mismatches} in the second. The cross-talk is therefore not inherited from the labels. We do not claim a cause of the loss on every surface. Component-level analysis, and interventions for any causal statement, have not been done.

\subsection{Held-out label recovery}\label{sec:res-recovery}

Every training pair of a country carries a label: the completion that goes with the sign of the country's GPS coordinate on the pair's own dimension is the chosen one. A construction check asks whether that installation carries over to pairs the adapters never saw. For each of the \dc{42}{countries} first-cohort adapters we scored the held-out pairs of the training split as the log probability of the chosen and of the rejected completion, under the adapter and under the base model, and call the difference the margin. Recovery is the share of held-out pairs on which the adapter's margin exceeds the base model's. Accuracy is the share on which the adapter prefers its own chosen completion outright, and base accuracy is the same share for the base model. We report country-equal means with a profile-block bootstrap.

Recovery is \cl{0.940}{C9_label_recovery_recovery_profile_block} \ci{0.926}{0.952}{C9_label_recovery_recovery_profile_block} and accuracy is \cl{0.816}{C9_label_recovery_accuracy_profile_block} \ci{0.771}{0.856}{C9_label_recovery_accuracy_profile_block}. The base model's accuracy is \cl{0.489}{C9_label_recovery_base_accuracy_profile_block} \ci{0.471}{0.506}{C9_label_recovery_base_accuracy_profile_block}, an interval that includes one half, so the base model shows no detectable preference for the chosen completions before training. The mean margin shift is \cl{40.3}{C9_label_recovery_mean_margin_shift_profile_block} \ci{28.2}{54.1}{C9_label_recovery_mean_margin_shift_profile_block} nats. These are construction checks. They show that training installed each country's synthetic labels on scenarios the adapter never saw. They say nothing about whether the labels order countries like the GPS and nothing about the respondents, and the distance results above show that the same adapters lie farther from the survey's answer distributions than the base model does.
