\begin{abstract}
\noindent A language model that answers survey items must hold at each link between its source and the people it describes. We evaluate one instrument along the chain from anchor to instrument to respondents: adapters compiled from declared country-level benchmark signs and scored on new items without naming the country. Training installs the synthetic labels on unseen pairs, and the adapters keep the benchmark's order of countries on trust, as does a prompt stating the same signs. The other dimensions are entangled. A preregistered screen of held-out items, against a retrospective human criterion, yields a weak signal that registered comparisons do not separate from readers of the questionnaire. The adapters' order of countries does not detectably agree with respondents, and their answer distributions lie farther from the survey than the base model's, whereas a country-named prompt lies closer with no trust order. The instrument can complement human pretesting; pre-piloting remains untested.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} synthetic respondents; large language models; measurement validity; survey item screening; preregistration; cross-national measurement.
