\begin{abstract}
\noindent Synthetic respondents built from large language models promise a cheap first reading of survey items, yet are rarely tested as screening instruments on items they were not built for. We evaluate synthetic cultural agents, which compile the signs of declared country-level preference benchmarks into parameter adapters through a regularized pairwise choice model, as a scoring instrument for new item text. Under a preregistered protocol, the adapters alone assigned a dimension and direction to candidate World Values Survey items before any human value was read. In a retrospective screen against the existing survey, selected items predict the direction of cross-national differences better than random assignment, but the signal is weak, and no dimension passes both a second-bank check and the screen. A factorial over sign profiles shows entangled dimensions, more so under prompting. Because the human criterion is retrospective, pre-piloting remains a prospective use that this paper does not test.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} synthetic respondents; large language models; survey item screening; preregistration; cross-national measurement; direct preference optimization.
