\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Synthetic cultural agents compile declared aggregate preference signs into choice policies that can be read on any survey item without naming a group. Used as a scoring instrument for new item text, they keep the direction of every declared sign, keep the trust encoding across a change of scenario bank, and produce a weak retrospective screening signal on held-out WVS items that exceeds random assignment. They do not keep the declared dimensions separate, no dimension passes both the second-bank check and the screen, and on the few items where comparison is possible the registered contrasts do not distinguish them from a careful reader of the questionnaire. Prompting a model with the same signs is more entangled than training and moves three of its own surfaces the wrong way.

A researcher may use the instrument now as an inspectable, group-level reading of candidate items beside human pretesting. The test that would license more is prospective: probe-selected items fielded to people after the selection and a human-only comparison rule have both been declared, so that the instrument's selections can be compared with what a researcher would otherwise have fielded. Until then, the instrument complements human pretesting and fielding.
