\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We construct synthetic cultural agents from declared aggregate preference anchors through a shared bank of sign-labeled comparisons. Joint adapter fitting and country-unconditioned scoring make the population-specific labeling rule inspectable. The resulting policy remains conditional on the pretrained model, bank semantics, and fitting procedure; it does not identify a human preference distribution.

In the GPS--WVS application, the adapters recover their synthetic labels and retain trust anchor ordering on new survey text more strongly than the country-named comparator. Human agreement remains uncertain, and the other dimensions provide mixed evidence. This distinction between anchor retention and survey prediction is central to interpreting the construction.

The prospective application is group-level survey pre-piloting and comparison of candidate indicators against declared anchors. Demonstrating that application requires an independent human item-screening study and stronger evidence of transport across banks and populations. Until then, the fitted policies are auditable scoring instruments intended to complement human survey research.
