\begin{abstract}
\noindent We evaluate sixteen country-specific DPO/QLoRA adapters (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, trained on synthetic GPS-sign preference pairs with no country tokens in any prompt and no WVS/AB data in training) against two inference-time alternatives on the same surface: the unconditioned base model and the base model prompted as a resident of each country, plus a uniform random baseline and the human population itself. Every model is passed through identical pipelines: distributional fit to WVS Wave-7 population response distributions (TVD, JSD, entropy and dispersion errors, top-option match), a construct bridge to GPS country $z$-scores, and nomological development restrictions (log GDP per capita, education partials, Gini). The results split cleanly: prompting recovers distributional shape (persona TVD 0.375 vs adapter 0.469 and base 0.443, with noise at 0.338 --- a diffuse-population floor), while only the trained adapters recover the GPS preference \emph{direction} and its economic gradient (adapter trust composite $\rho_{\mathrm{GPS}}=0.78$ vs persona 0.06; base and noise are single fixed distributions whose cross-country correlations are undefined by construction). CF\_ST cultural distance (Muthukrishna et al.\ 2020) and a PCA of item \emph{means} disagree because they measure different objects: frequency/shape versus location. We discuss what researchers gain from a weights-only, aggregate-anchor policy, why soft DPO is the wrong next lever if the goal is histogram fit, and which venues the evidence can honestly support.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data and construction of every number}\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Models evaluated}
\label{sec:models}
For each of sixteen countries ($\mathcal{C}$ = CHN, JPN, GBR, USA, MEX, ARG, DEU, RUS, IND, IDN, NGA, EGY, TUR, NLD, BRA, GRC) we evaluate four model distributions plus two references:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{base} --- Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, unconditioned prompt (``Answer this questionnaire as an individual person''). Option probabilities computed as softmax over summed per-token log-probabilities of each response code completion, exactly as in the canonical evaluation notebook. Because the prompt never names a country, base is a \emph{single fixed distribution} replicated across countries; only its per-country TVD (against each country's population) varies.
\item \textbf{persona} --- the same base model with the country named in the prompt (``Answer this questionnaire as a typical adult living in COUNTRY''). Scored identically; this produces sixteen \emph{distinct} distributions, one per prompted country. Run 2026-08-19, T4, 5{,}616 option rows.
\item \textbf{adapter (per country)} --- the SCA2 DPO/QLoRA adapter for that country, unconditioned prompt. Trained on 526 synthetic GPS-sign pairs (658-pair production bank, $G=\mathrm{sign}(z)$ labels), 1 epoch, $\beta=0.1$, LoRA $r{=}16/\alpha{=}32$. Weights-only: the country signal must live in the LoRA weights.
\item \textbf{noise} --- uniform distribution over the population option grid for each (country, item). This is the random-guess floor.
\item \textbf{human (WVS)} --- the survey-weighted population response distribution (the target; by construction all fit metrics are 0 for it).
\item \textbf{human (GPS)} --- the GPS country $z$-scores (76 countries; the anchor source).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The evaluation surface}
\label{sec:surface}
All models are scored on the same 35 WVS Wave-7 items (the question map in \texttt{DPO\_eval\_WVS/question\_map\_wvs\_edited.csv}). The \textbf{distributional surface} uses the 30 single-choice items (binary + ordered scales); the 3 multi-select and 2 numeric-open items are excluded because their option space is not a single softmax. The construct and development surfaces use the 30-item map through dimension composites.

\subsection{Metric definitions (how every number is built)}
\label{sec:metrics}
For a model's option distribution $p$ and the population distribution $q$ on a common option grid $\{v_k\}$ (both normalized to sum 1):
\begin{align}
\mathrm{TVD} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_k |p_k-q_k|, &
\mathrm{JSD} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_k p_k\log_2\tfrac{p_k}{m_k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_k q_k\log_2\tfrac{q_k}{m_k},\ \ m_k=\tfrac{p_k+q_k}{2}, \nonumber\\[3pt]
\mathrm{EntErr} &= H(p)-H(q), &
\mathrm{StdErr} &= \sigma(p)-\sigma(q),\ \ H(p)=-\textstyle\sum_k p_k\log_2 p_k, \nonumber\\[3pt]
\mathrm{MeanErr} &= \mu(p)-\mu(q), &
\mathrm{TopMatch} &= \mathbf{1}\bigl[\arg\max_k p_k=\arg\max_k q_k\bigr]. \nonumber
\end{align}

\textbf{Composites.} For each dimension $d \in \{$trust, patience, risktaking, positive reciprocity, negative reciprocity, altruism$\}$, the country-level composite is the mean over that dimension's mapped items of the item means, after polarity recoding so that higher = more of the construct (trust items $5-v$ on 1--4 scales; justifiability $11-v$ on 1--10; binary items recoded \emph{option-wise} as $1\{v=1\}$ then averaged, never recoded as $1\{\mathrm{mean}=1\}$). Human composites are survey-weighted; model composites weight each recoded option by the model's probability. Missing WVS codes (negative values) are masked \emph{before} recoding. These recodes match \texttt{analysis/phase2/06\_construct\_bridge.py} on the human layer.

\textbf{Construct bridge.} Spearman correlation across countries between the country composite and the GPS country $z$-score for that dimension. Human layer: 42 countries with both WVS and GPS. Adapter and persona layers: 16 countries. \textbf{Base and noise are excluded}: as single fixed (or grid-uniform) distributions they have no cross-country composite variation, so their Spearman is undefined (marked ``---'').

\textbf{Development restrictions.} Spearman correlation across countries between the composite and $\log$ GDP per capita (World Bank WDI \texttt{NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD}, PPP constant 2017 US\$, 2015--2019 mean; \texttt{data/phase2/aux/wdi.csv}). Partial Spearman controls education (Q275, WVS ISCED, survey-weighted country mean). Gini (WDI \texttt{SI.POV.GINI}) is reported as a secondary auxiliary. Base and noise are again excluded (undefined).

\textbf{Direction.} Top-option match rate by dimension: the fraction of items in the dimension where the model's modal answer equals the population's modal answer.

\textbf{The noise floor is informative.} Uniform noise has TVD 0.338 --- \emph{lower} than every model's TVD. The WVS populations are so diffuse (multi-modal, near-uniform on many items) that any concentrated distribution pays a TVD penalty. TVD therefore rewards \emph{flatness} as much as accuracy, and model-vs-model TVD comparisons must be read against this floor: persona (0.375) is only 0.037 above noise; the adapters (0.469) are 0.13 above.

\textbf{Persona is the best model on the distributional surface.} Persona TVD 0.375 $<$ base 0.443 $<$ adapters 0.469, and persona also has the smallest under-dispersion (entropy error $-0.44$ vs base $-0.54$ and adapters $-0.74$) and the best top-option match (0.48 vs 0.45/0.45). Persona beats the adapter in 15/16 countries and the base in 15/16 (Fig.~\ref{fig:dist}; GRC is the adapter win on TVD).

\textbf{Why adapters underperform on TVD.} The adapters are the most over-concentrated family (entropy error $-0.74$, std error $-0.65$), a direct consequence of hard pairwise DPO labels: a pairwise preference signal identifies an ordering, never population marginals. This replicates the mode-collapse diagnosis of Pfeifer \& Dalloul (2026) on the same base model.

\textbf{The key contrast.} On trust, the adapter composite ranks countries in GPS order ($\rho=0.78$), the human WVS layer does too ($0.41$, consistent with Falk et al.\ 2018's $0.49$), but the persona prompt does \emph{not} ($0.06$). \textbf{The imprint pattern on exploratory dimensions.} Risktaking, positive reciprocity, and altruism show adapter $\rho$ larger than the human layer's (risktaking $0.57$ vs $-0.08$). Because those WVS items do not track GPS in humans, a high adapter $\rho$ there is the GPS sign bit expressed through a poorly mapped instrument --- an imprint, not a recovered survey construct. The honest reading of the bridge: only trust and patience are real GPS proxies in this menu. Adapters transfer trust cleanly; patience is transferred at human-instrument strength; the rest is imprint.

\section{Why CF\_ST and PCA put the adapters in different places}
\label{sec:cfst}

The two maps in Fig.~\ref{fig:maps} look like they contradict each other. They do not, once the metric is written down. They also looked worse than they should have in the 19 August draft because of the concatenation bug in Section~\ref{sec:figures}; the geometry below is from the rebuilt files.

\subsection{What CF\_ST is}
Muthukrishna et al.\ (2020) apply the population-genetic fixation index to survey traits: questions are loci, answers are alleles. For two populations $A$ and $B$ on one item with option values $v$ and probabilities $p^A$, $p^B$,
\begin{align}
\mu_A &= \textstyle\sum_k v_k p^A_k,\qquad
\mu_B = \textstyle\sum_k v_k p^B_k,\qquad
\bar\mu = (\mu_A+\mu_B)/2, \nonumber\\
\sigma^2_G &= \tfrac12\bigl[(\mu_A-\bar\mu)^2+(\mu_B-\bar\mu)^2\bigr], \nonumber\\
\sigma^2_T &= \tfrac12\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_k p^A_k(v_k-\bar\mu)^2 + \textstyle\sum_k p^B_k(v_k-\bar\mu)^2\Bigr], \nonumber\\
F_{\mathrm{ST}} &= \sigma^2_G / \sigma^2_T \quad (\text{0 if }\sigma^2_T\approx 0). \nonumber
\end{align}
Pairwise cultural distance is the mean $F_{\mathrm{ST}}$ over the 30 mapped items, after aligning option grids. $F_{\mathrm{ST}}=0$ if the two response \emph{distributions} are identical; $F_{\mathrm{ST}}=1$ if they are maximally separated. Because $\sigma^2_T$ includes within-group spread, two populations with the same mean but different frequencies (one peaked, one flat; one bimodal, one unimodal) are far apart. That is the point of the metric, and Muthukrishna et al.'s explicit criticism of Hofstede/Kogut--Singh mean-distance measures.

The map in Fig.~\ref{fig:maps}(a) is classical MDS of the joint $42+16+16+2$ distance matrix (countries, adapters, personas, base, noise). MDS places two points close only if their full option-frequency vectors are close.

\subsection{What the PCA map is}
Fig.~\ref{fig:maps}(b) is not a cultural-distance embedding. It is a two-axis SVD of the $42\times 30$ matrix of \emph{human recoded item means}, with model composites projected onto those axes after subtracting the human column means. Two countries are close if their 30-vector of means is close. Dispersion, bimodality, and which option carries the mass are invisible.

\subsection{Why the locations differ}
Hard DPO produces over-concentrated output (entropy error $-0.74$). CF\_ST treats that as a large distance to every human population, because humans are diffuse: 11/16 adapters are nearest to Canada, a mid-scale attractor, and the median own-country rank is $18.5$ of 42 (chance $21.5$). PCA only sees the mean, so an adapter can sit nearer a Western cluster on means while remaining far from every country on frequencies. That is Fig.~\ref{fig:maps}(b)'s upper-right adapter cloud versus Fig.~\ref{fig:maps}(a)'s adapter island. Fig.~\ref{fig:geocomp} makes the disagreement quantitative: only BRA is close on both maps; GBR and USA rank 2 on CF\_ST because they sit near CAN, not because they recovered those countries.

The scientific implication is narrow and useful. CF\_ST is the right metric for the H2 claim (``do we reproduce population histograms?''). PCA of means is a picture of the H1/construct-bridge claim (``do country orderings of averages track GPS?''). Using one map to argue the other claim is a category error. We keep both, with this section as the caption.

\section{Interpretation: what do researchers and policy-makers gain?}
\label{sec:gains}

Our current method does \emph{not} deliver better distributional fit than a country-named prompt, and both are only marginally better than uniform noise on TVD. The contribution is therefore \emph{not} ``we reproduce WVS histograms.'' It is the following, each point anchored in the results above.

\paragraph{1. Weights-only preference direction, with zero microdata.}
The adapters recover the GPS \emph{direction} (held-out pair accuracy $0.89$--$0.99$, $n=132$ per country) and the GPS \emph{ordering} of countries on trust ($\rho=0.78$) without a country token in any prompt and without any individual survey microdata (training used only country-level GPS aggregates through $G=\mathrm{sign}(z)$ labels). This is the anti-leakage claim: the country signal demonstrably lives in the weights. A researcher who needs a \emph{policy} whose preference direction is identified as trained-in --- for instrument pre-testing, for power analyses that need a signed preference, for counterfactuals that must not smuggle a country name --- can use these adapters with a known provenance. Prompting is an opaque inference-time manipulation of the same base.

\paragraph{2. A nomological anchor that prompting cannot provide.}
The adapters satisfy the development restriction in sign (trust/patience rise with $\log$ GDP pc, education partials $+0.50$/$+0.46$) \emph{and} the construct bridge on trust, while the persona prompt fails the trust bridge ($\rho=0.06$) and the base model provides no country-level information at all on these surfaces. For a policy-maker asking ``does this simulated population carry the same economic gradient as the real one?,'' only the adapter is even a candidate --- and the gradient is weak, not a substitute for human data.

\paragraph{3. The dispersion problem is measured, not hidden.}
Pairwise DPO cannot identify population marginals, producing over-concentration (entropy error $-0.74$). Temperature scaling at inference recovers most of the shape (pooled TVD $0.47\to 0.33$ at $T{=}3$, top-option match unchanged) without touching location. The shape loss is a softmax artifact; the location loss is structural.

\paragraph{4. Sign-twins are a development shadow.}
The 76-country bank collapses to 35 sign profiles; twin proximity on human WVS is explained by shared GDP (partial $\rho=-0.09$). Country-level sign patterns are not cultural families.

\paragraph{What we cannot claim.}
We cannot claim histogram reproduction, country-specific dispersion, or magnitudes. The persona baseline quantifies the anti-leakage price at $\sim$0.09 TVD on average. Anyone using these adapters for point prediction of survey distributions should not; anyone using them for preference \emph{direction} with transparent provenance can.

\section{Should we consider soft DPO, or Ksennia's extended-prompt bank?}
\label{sec:softdpo}

Direction is already captured on the GPS pairs (H1: $0.89$--$0.99$). The remaining failure is H2: population histograms. Two candidate levers were discussed with Ksennia on 19 August: (i) \emph{soft} / intensity-weighted DPO, and (ii) an \emph{extended-prompt} training bank that is not binary yes/no.

\textbf{Soft DPO / intensity weights} (preference-strength as a function of $|z|$; related to Sharifnassab et al.\ 2024 SPO, not Wu et al.\ ICML 2024, which is a different method). This addresses knife-edge sign labels (patience/risktaking near-zero $z$) by down-weighting weak pairs. It does \emph{not} identify population marginals: a pairwise loss, weighted or not, still rewards concentrating mass on the chosen completion. It cannot fix NLD's directional collapse, which came from \emph{strong} signs that oppose that population's WVS. Compute is essentially the current 30-minute adapter. Expected gain: patience/risktaking \emph{direction}, not TVD.

\textbf{Extended-prompt bank.} Prior runs used richer scenario completions; the current bank is binary A/B. Ksennia's estimate is $\sim$2 hours per adapter versus $\sim$30 minutes. This is the only training-side lever that can change the \emph{shape} of the learned distribution without touching WVS, because the model is no longer forced to put mass on a two-point support. It still will not encode base rates unless the completions themselves carry graded intensity. It is also the only lever that tests her qualitative claim that ``binary-only training limits nuance.''

\textbf{Already measured, do not re-spend GPU on.} Inference-time temperature scaling already moves TVD $0.47\to 0.33$ without moving modes. Country-named prompting already beats adapters on TVD by $0.09$. Any histogram-fit objective that is allowed to use a country token should just use the persona baseline.

\textbf{What we will not do.} KL-to-WVS, moment matching, or any loss that trains on population marginals. That contaminates the out-of-sample design --- the paper's core claim is that WVS stays unseen.

\textbf{Recommendation, in order.} (1) Keep temperature scaling as the reported inference diagnostic, not a training change. (2) Run the extended-prompt bank on a 4-country subset (one confirmatory trust/patience pair, one exploratory, one NLD-like conflict, one sign-twin) before any 16-country retrain --- this is the only test of whether richer completions move H2 when H1 is already in hand. (3) Soft DPO / $|z|<0.1$ pruning on the \emph{frozen} bank as a cheap ablation for knife-edge dimensions, not as a histogram fix. If the 4-country extended-prompt subset does not move matched TVD by a predeclared margin (we suggest $0.05$ below current adapter TVD, still above noise is acceptable), stop; the pairwise operator has hit its identification bound.

\section{Journal landscape for the work as it stands}
\label{sec:journals}
The paper is a constructive methodology with an honest negative result on histograms, not a ``we reproduce WVS'' claim. Prestigious general-science journals (Nature, Science, PNAS research article) will desk-reject a 16-country Llama-3.1 adapter study whose primary external metric loses to a one-line country prompt and to uniform noise. That is the right desk rejection: the evidence does not yet support ``LLMs can stand in for populations.''

What the evidence \emph{does} support is a methods paper that changes the question from impersonation to construction, reports the weights-versus-prompt dissociation, and treats distributional failure as a designed identification bound. That is a better paper than a weaker claim in \emph{Review of Experimental Economics}, and it is the only honest path above field-journal default.

A feasible ladder, given current results:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Nature Human Behaviour or Science Advances, Article / Registered Report} --- only if the next wave adds (a) the 4-country extended-prompt subset with a predeclared TVD margin, (b) a restricted permutation placebo, and (c) a non-LLM aggregate baseline, and the write-up leads with the protocol and the bound, not with $\rho=0.78$. NHB format is $\le 5{,}000$ words excluding Methods. Current 16-country tables become Extended Data. This is the stretch goal, not the submission this month.
\item \textbf{PNAS Nexus} --- the realistic prestigious target now. Tao et al.\ (2024) already published cultural-alignment evaluation there; a constructive counterpart with anti-leakage, zero microdata, and an honest noise floor is in-scope. Interdisciplinary, accepts methods with limitations, preprint-friendly.
\item \textbf{Sociological Methods \& Research} or \textbf{Political Analysis} --- the synthetic-survey critique literature lives here (Bisbee et al.\ 2024; Boelaert et al.\ 2025). Strongest fit for the negative H2 result plus the persona price. Field prestige is high; general-science reach is not.
\item \textbf{Computational Linguistics / ACL Findings} --- if the contribution is framed as operator identification (pairwise DPO cannot recover marginals; temperature vs prompt vs weights). Competes with SubPOP/Krsteski; they use real microdata, we do not. That contrast is the paper.
\item \textbf{Review of Experimental Economics} --- SCA1's home. Correct as a fallback, too small as a first try if the lab wants a more prestigious outlet than experimental economics. Do not submit there first solely because SCA1 did.
\end{itemize}

The pitch that fails: ``adapters beat the base on WVS.'' They do not, on TVD. The pitch that can work: ``here is what a weights-only, zero-microdata, aggregate-anchor policy is and is not identified for, with a country-prompt and a noise floor as mandatory baselines.'' Temperature scaling, the construct bridge, and the CF\_ST/PCA distinction are the quantitative spine.

\section{Status of contributions and next steps}
\label{sec:next}
\paragraph{Current status.}
(i) H1: direction recovered on GPS pairs ($0.89$--$0.99$). (ii) Construct bridge: trust/patience are the real proxies; adapters transfer trust ordering ($\rho=0.78$ / $0.42$); persona does not on trust ($0.06$ / $0.60$). (iii) Development: adapters satisfy the GPS gradient in sign (partials $+0.50$/$+0.46$); persona overshoots; base undefined. (iv) No distributional specificity: CF\_ST own-country median rank $18.5$ of 42 (chance $21.5$); 11/16 nearest to CAN; sign-twins are a development shadow. (v) Temperature fixes shape, not location ($0.47\to 0.33$ TVD; top-match unchanged). (vi) Trust over-generalization: family--outgroup gradient compressed $3.7\times$ in 16/16 countries. (vii) Persona baseline: prompting beats adapters on TVD in 15/16 countries, fails the trust bridge --- the anti-leakage price and the weights-vs-prompt dissociation are both quantified. (viii) Audit this round: binary recode-of-mean and CF\_ST grid-concatenation bugs fixed; maps rebuilt; Wu (2024) citation corrected to Sharifnassab et al.\ (2024) for SPO.

\paragraph{Next steps (priority order).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Extended-prompt 4-country subset} (the Ksennia lever that can still move H2 if direction is already in hand). Predeclare a TVD margin. Do not retrain all 16 until the subset moves.
\item \textbf{Soft DPO / $|z|<0.1$ prune on the frozen bank} --- cheap, tests knife-edge labels, not expected to fix dispersion. Run after or in parallel with (1), never instead of it if the goal is histogram fit.
\item \textbf{Restricted permutation placebo} on the same 4-country subset --- without it, H1 recovery is still ``generic synthetic tuning'' as an alternative explanation.
\item \textbf{OOS on a second instrument} (AmericasBarometer, USA/MEX locally) for the construct bridge, not for TVD heroics.
\item \textbf{Keep the persona run as a committed baseline} in every table. Game-theoretic scenarios and a new cvprofiles network are useful later; they are not what decides whether the pairwise operator has hit its bound.
\end{enumerate}
