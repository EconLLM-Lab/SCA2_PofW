\begin{abstract}
\noindent We evaluate sixteen country-specific DPO/QLoRA adapters (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, trained on synthetic GPS-sign preference pairs with no country tokens in any prompt and no WVS/AB data in training) against two inference-time alternatives on the same surface: the unconditioned base model and the base model prompted as a resident of each country, plus a uniform random baseline and the human population itself. Every model is passed through identical pipelines: distributional fit to WVS Wave-7 population response distributions (TVD, JSD, entropy and dispersion errors, top-option match), a construct bridge to GPS country $z$-scores, and nomological development restrictions (log GDP per capita, education partials, Gini). The results split cleanly: prompting recovers distributional shape (persona TVD 0.375 vs adapter 0.469 and base 0.443, with noise at 0.338 --- a diffuse-population floor), while only the trained adapters recover the GPS preference \emph{direction} and its economic gradient (adapter trust composite $\rho_{\mathrm{GPS}}=0.70$ vs persona 0.06; base and noise are single fixed distributions whose cross-country correlations are undefined by construction). We discuss what researchers and policy-makers gain from a weights-only, aggregate-anchor policy, whether soft DPO is warranted, and the journal landscape for the work as it stands.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data and construction of every number}\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Models evaluated}
\label{sec:models}
For each of sixteen countries ($\mathcal{C}$ = CHN, JPN, GBR, USA, MEX, ARG, DEU, RUS, IND, IDN, NGA, EGY, TUR, NLD, BRA, GRC) we evaluate four model distributions plus two references:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{base} --- Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, unconditioned prompt (``Answer this questionnaire as an individual person''). Option probabilities computed as softmax over summed per-token log-probabilities of each response code completion, exactly as in the canonical evaluation notebook. Because the prompt never names a country, base is a \emph{single fixed distribution} replicated across countries; only its per-country TVD (against each country's population) varies.
\item \textbf{persona} --- the same base model with the country named in the prompt (``Answer this questionnaire as a typical adult living in COUNTRY''). Scored identically; this produces sixteen \emph{distinct} distributions, one per prompted country. New run, 2026-08-19, T4, 5{,}616 option rows.
\item \textbf{adapter (per country)} --- the SCA2 DPO/QLoRA adapter for that country, unconditioned prompt. Trained on 526 synthetic GPS-sign pairs (658-pair production bank, $G=\mathrm{sign}(z)$ labels), 1 epoch, $\beta=0.1$, LoRA $r{=}16/\alpha{=}32$. Weights-only: the country signal must live in the LoRA weights.
\item \textbf{noise} --- uniform distribution over the population option grid for each (country, item). This is the random-guess floor.
\item \textbf{human (WVS)} --- the survey-weighted population response distribution (the target; by construction all fit metrics are 0 for it).
\item \textbf{human (GPS)} --- the GPS country $z$-scores (76 countries; the anchor source).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The evaluation surface}
\label{sec:surface}
All models are scored on the same 35 WVS Wave-7 items (the question map in \texttt{DPO\_eval\_WVS/question\_map\_wvs\_edited.csv}). The \textbf{distributional surface} uses the 30 single-choice items (binary + ordered scales); the 3 multi-select and 2 numeric-open items are excluded because their option space is not a single softmax. The construct and development surfaces use the full 30-item map through dimension composites.

\subsection{Metric definitions (how every number is built)}
\label{sec:metrics}
For a model's option distribution $p$ and the population distribution $q$ on a common option grid $\{v_k\}$ (both normalized to sum 1):
\begin{align}
\mathrm{TVD} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_k |p_k-q_k|, &
\mathrm{JSD} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_k p_k\log_2\tfrac{p_k}{m_k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_k q_k\log_2\tfrac{q_k}{m_k},\ \ m_k=\tfrac{p_k+q_k}{2}, \nonumber\\[3pt]
\mathrm{EntErr} &= H(p)-H(q), &
\mathrm{StdErr} &= \sigma(p)-\sigma(q),\ \ H(p)=-\textstyle\sum_k p_k\log_2 p_k, \nonumber\\[3pt]
\mathrm{MeanErr} &= \mu(p)-\mu(q), &
\mathrm{TopMatch} &= \mathbf{1}\bigl[\arg\max_k p_k=\arg\max_k q_k\bigr]. \nonumber
\end{align}

\textbf{Composites.} For each dimension $d \in \{$trust, patience, risktaking, positive reciprocity, negative reciprocity, altruism$\}$, the country-level composite is the mean over that dimension's mapped items of the item means, after polarity recoding so that higher = more of the construct (trust items $5-v$ on 1--4 scales; justifiability $11-v$ on 1--10; binary trust $1$ if trust, $0$ otherwise). Human composites are survey-weighted; model composites weight each option by the model's probability. Missing WVS codes (negative values) are masked \emph{before} recoding. These recodes are identical to \texttt{analysis/phase2/06\_construct\_bridge.py}.

\textbf{Construct bridge.} Spearman correlation across countries between the country composite and the GPS country $z$-score for that dimension. Human layer: 42 countries with both WVS and GPS. Adapter and persona layers: 16 countries. \textbf{Base and noise are excluded}: as single fixed (or grid-uniform) distributions they have no cross-country composite variation, so their Spearman is undefined (marked ``---'').

\textbf{Development restrictions.} Spearman correlation across countries between the composite and $\log$ GDP per capita (World Bank WDI \texttt{NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD}, PPP constant 2017 US\$, 2015--2019 mean; \texttt{data/phase2/aux/wdi.csv}). Partial Spearman controls education (Q275, WVS ISCED, survey-weighted country mean). Gini (WDI \texttt{SI.POV.GINI}) is reported as a secondary auxiliary. These follow the cvprofiles convention ($\beta = $ coefficient on $\log$ GDP per capita controlling education). Base and noise are again excluded (undefined).

\textbf{Direction.} Top-option match rate by dimension: the fraction of items in the dimension where the model's modal answer equals the population's modal answer.

\textbf{The noise floor is informative.} Uniform noise has TVD 0.338 --- \emph{lower} than every model's TVD. The WVS populations are so diffuse (multi-modal, near-uniform on many items) that any concentrated distribution pays a TVD penalty. TVD therefore rewards \emph{flatness} as much as accuracy, and model-vs-model TVD comparisons must be read against this floor: persona (0.375) is only 0.037 above noise; the adapters (0.469) are 0.13 above.

\textbf{Persona is the best model on the distributional surface.} Persona TVD 0.375 < base 0.443 < adapters 0.469, and persona also has the smallest under-dispersion (entropy error $-0.44$ vs base $-0.54$ and adapters $-0.74$) and the best top-option match (0.48 vs 0.45/0.45). Persona beats the adapter in 15/16 countries and the base in 15/16 (Fig.~\ref{fig:dist}).

\textbf{Why adapters underperform on TVD.} The adapters are the most over-concentrated family (entropy error $-0.74$, std error $-0.65$), a direct consequence of hard pairwise DPO labels: a pairwise preference signal identifies an ordering, never population marginals. This replicates the mode-collapse diagnosis of Pfeifer \& Dalloul (2026) on the same base model, and of the wider synthetic-survey literature.

\textbf{The key contrast.} On trust, the adapter composite ranks countries in GPS order ($\rho=0.70$), the human WVS layer does too ($0.41$, consistent with Falk et al.\ 2018's $0.49$), but the persona prompt does \emph{not} ($0.06$). The unconditioned base cannot be assessed at all (no country variation). The same pattern holds for patience (adapter 0.42, human 0.32, persona 0.60) and risktaking (adapter 0.57 vs human $-0.08$). \textbf{The GPS preference ordering lives in the trained weights, not in the country-named prompt.} The persona prompt buys distributional shape; it does not reproduce the GPS construct ranking.

\textbf{The imprint pattern on exploratory dimensions.} Risktaking, positive reciprocity, and altruism show adapter $\rho$ substantially \emph{larger} than the human layer's (e.g.\ risktaking 0.57 vs $-0.08$). Because those WVS items do not track GPS in humans, a high adapter $\rho$ there is the GPS sign bit expressed through a poorly mapped instrument --- an imprint, not a recovered survey construct. The honest reading of the bridge: only trust and patience are real GPS proxies in this menu, and the adapters transfer exactly those.

\section{Interpretation: what do researchers and policy-makers gain?}
\label{sec:gains}

Our current method does \emph{not} deliver better distributional fit than a country-named prompt, and both are only marginally better than uniform noise on TVD. The contribution is therefore \emph{not} ``we reproduce WVS histograms.'' It is the following, each point anchored in the results above.

\paragraph{1. Weights-only preference direction, with zero microdata.}
The adapters recover the GPS \emph{direction} (held-out pair accuracy 0.89--0.99, n=132 per country) and the GPS \emph{ordering} of countries on trust and patience ($\rho=0.70$ and 0.42), \emph{without} a country token in any prompt and \emph{without} any individual survey microdata (training used only country-level GPS aggregates through $G=\mathrm{sign}(z)$ labels). This is the anti-leakage claim: the country signal demonstrably lives in the weights. A researcher who needs a \emph{policy} whose preference direction is identified as trained-in --- for counterfactual ``what if this country's preferences'' simulations, for pre-testing instruments, for power analyses --- can use these adapters with a known provenance, whereas prompting is an opaque inference-time manipulation of the same base.

\paragraph{2. A nomological anchor that prompting cannot provide.}
The adapters satisfy the development restriction (trust/patience rise with $\log$ GDP pc, education partials $+0.56$/$+0.46$) \emph{and} the construct bridge, while the persona prompt fails the bridge and the base model provides no country-level information at all on these surfaces. For a policy-maker asking ``does this simulated population carry the same economic gradient as the real one?,'' only the adapter passes.

\paragraph{3. The dispersion problem is measured, not hidden.}
We quantify exactly where the method fails: pairwise DPO cannot identify population marginals, producing over-concentration (entropy error $-0.74$). Temperature scaling at inference recovers most of the shape (pooled TVD 0.47$\rightarrow$0.33 at $T{=}3$, top-option match unchanged) without touching location. This is a published-diagnostic-grade finding: the shape loss is a softmax artifact, the location loss is structural.

\paragraph{4. Sign-twins are a development shadow.}
The 76-country bank collapses to 35 sign profiles; twin proximity on human WVS is explained by shared GDP (partial $\rho=-0.09$). This is a warning to the field: country-level sign patterns are not cultural families.

\paragraph{What we cannot claim.}
We cannot claim histogram reproduction, country-specific dispersion, or magnitudes. The persona baseline quantifies the anti-leakage price at $\sim$0.09 TVD on average. Anyone using these adapters for point prediction of survey distributions should not; anyone using them for preference \emph{direction} with transparent provenance can.

\section{Should we consider soft DPO?}
\label{sec:softdpo}
Soft DPO (preference-intensity weighting, e.g.\ Wu et al.\ 2024) addresses exactly one limitation: knife-edge sign labels (patience/risktaking near-zero $z$) by down-weighting weak-preference pairs. It does \emph{not} address the two drivers of the TVD loss: under-dispersion (a pairwise loss never identifies marginals, weighted or not) and level shifts (weighting never encodes base rates). It also cannot fix NLD's directional collapse, which came from strong-sign labels opposing that population's WVS. And any fix that trains on population marginals (KL-to-WVS, moment matching) would contaminate the out-of-sample design --- the paper's core claim is that WVS stays unseen. The legitimate levers are synthetic-side shape (richer multi-option completions), inference-side temperature scaling (verified: fixes shape without touching location), and label-side pruning ($|z|<0.1$ drop). Soft DPO is a designed follow-up, not a fix.

\section{Journal landscape for the work as it stands}
\label{sec:journals}
As it stands, the paper is a \emph{constructive methodology + honest negative-result} contribution, not a ``we reproduce WVS'' claim. The strongest fit is a methods-first venue that rewards identification discipline and accepts negative results:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Review of Experimental Economics} (the SCA1 home; natural first stop for the lab's line).
\item \textbf{Sociological Methods \& Research} or \textbf{Political Analysis} (the synthetic-survey critique literature lives here: Bisbee et al.\ 2024, Boelaert et al.\ 2025).
\item \textbf{Computational Linguistics / ACL Findings} (methodology + evaluation; the SubPOP/Krsteski crowd).
\item \textbf{PLOS ONE / PNAS Nexus} (broad multidisciplinary, accepts method papers with clear limitations).
\item \textbf{Nature Human Behaviour / Science Advances} --- only if reframed as the constructive SCA methodology paper (weights-only anchors, zero microdata, anti-leakage) with the distributional failure as a designed boundary, not a bug.
\end{itemize}

The current evidence does not yet support a Nature/PNAS-level submission as-is; the negative distributional result is the honest centerpiece, and the journal pitch must be ``here is what a weights-only, zero-microdata policy is and is not identified for,'' with the temperature-scaling and development analyses as the quantitative spine.

\section{Status of contributions and next steps}
\label{sec:next}
\paragraph{Current status.}
(i) H1: direction recovered on GPS pairs (0.89--0.99). (ii) Construct bridge: trust/patience are the real proxies; adapters transfer their ordering ($\rho=0.70$/0.42); persona does not ($0.06$/0.60). (iii) Development: adapters satisfy the GPS gradient (partials $+0.56$/$+0.46$); persona overshoots; base undefined. (iv) No distributional specificity: CF\_ST rank $\approx$ chance; sign-twins are a development shadow. (v) Temperature fixes shape, not location. (vi) Trust over-generalization: family--outgroup gradient compressed 3.7$\times$. (vii) Persona baseline: prompting beats adapters on TVD, fails the construct bridge --- the anti-leakage price and the weights-vs-prompt dissociation are both now quantified.

\paragraph{Next steps (priority order).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Game-theoretic scenario evaluation:} score adapters on trust, patience, altruism, and risk-taking game-theoretic scenarios (public-goods, ultimatum, time-preference tasks) against GPS and WVS benchmarks; the adapters' construct direction should survive, the dispersion boundary should be measured again.
\item \textbf{OOS evaluation on non-WVS surveys:} AmericasBarometer (USA/MEX locally) and a second instrument; tests whether the construct bridge transfers beyond the 30-item map.
\item \textbf{New nomological restrictions with cvprofiles:} add rule-of-law, inequality (Gini), and institutional quality restrictions to the trust network; compute the construct-identified range $[L,U]$ for trust using the adapter composites as candidate measures --- the partial-identification instrument makes the adapter-vs-human disagreement itself the measurement conclusion.
\item \textbf{Soft DPO / label-pruning ablation:} $|z|<0.1$ threshold and preference-intensity weights on the frozen bank; expected to improve patience/risktaking direction, not dispersion.
\item \textbf{Country-named prompt as a documented baseline:} the persona run is now a committed artifact; report it in every table and figure as the literature-comparison anchor.
\end{enumerate}
